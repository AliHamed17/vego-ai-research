# VEGO-AI Human-Judgment Literature

A curated, individually-verified reading list for the thesis question *"How can human judgment be captured, governed, and used to support agentic-AI-driven variability exploration in guideline operationalization scenarios, enabling reliable human–AI co-reasoning?"*

Format inspired by [Awesome-Human-Agent-Collaboration-Interaction-Systems](https://github.com/HenryPengZou/Awesome-Human-Agent-Collaboration-Interaction-Systems), shared by Prof. Iris Reinhartz-Berger during the 2026-08-05 supervisor call, in reply to [Zou et al. 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1811/), which Ali shared in the same chat. That survey is itself entry #1 below, tagged `foundation`.

**Status:** 140 sources, individually checked against a publisher page, DOI, dblp or arXiv record — 129 ✅ verified online, 10 ⚠️ partially verified, 1 ❌ could not be verified (quarantined below, never cited elsewhere). **The five frozen protocol searches (QL-01–QL-05) have not been executed** — this index shows what has been found and read, never that nothing else exists.

- ✅ **[2025]** [Leveraging machines to derive domain models from user stories](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00766-025-00442-9) — Maxim Bragilovski et al.. An end-to-end machine-learning pipeline, built via deliberate feature engineering, that derives both classes and associations for a domain model from a collection of user stories - with an open replication package of code, results and datasets.

Systematic comparative evaluation of multiple LLMs on automated domain modelling, establishing the capability envelope and the evaluation metrics (element-level precision/recall against reference models) that the subfield has largely adopted since.

An automated grading approach for UML class diagrams that maps student model elements onto an instructor's reference solution using syntactic and semantic similarity, then assigns marks from a rubric - motivated by consistency, fairness and scale in large classes.
- ✅ **[2017]** [Business Process Variability Modeling: A Survey](https://doi.org/10.1145/3041957) — Marcello La Rosa et al.. Systematic inventory and comparative evaluation of customisable process modelling approaches, with criteria for selecting among them and an explicit statement of gaps in the state of the art; motivated by organisations maintaining families of process variants that conventional BPM languages cannot express.
- ✅ **[2014]** [Software product line engineering and variability management: achievements and challenges](https://doi.org/10.1145/2593882.2593888) — Andreas Metzger et al.. Authoritative roadmap paper summarising SPLE research achievements against a standard product-line framework, identifying variability modelling and (informally) verified product-line artefacts as the strongest results, and naming the open challenges and intersections (services, cloud, big data, autonomic and adaptive systems).
- ⚠️ **[2014 (ref says 2013)]** [Variability in Software Systems - A Systematic Literature Review](https://doi.org/10.1109/TSE.2013.56) — Matthias Galster et al.. The canonical SLR on variability in software systems: 15,430 papers screened across 13 journals and 18 conferences, 196 analysed.
- ✅ **[2008]** [A Diagnosis Method that Matches Class Diagrams in a Learning Environment for Object-Oriented Modeling](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICALT.2008.187) — Ludovic Auxepaules et al.. A graph-matching diagnosis algorithm inside the Diagram learning environment that compares a student's UML class diagram against an expert reference diagram, schematising both into characteristic structural patterns to identify differences and drive pedagogical feedback.
- ✅ **[2005]** [Software Product Line Engineering: Foundations, Principles and Techniques](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-28901-1 (ISBN 978-3-540-24372-4)) — Klaus Pohl et al.. The standard textbook defining the SPLE framework: the split between domain engineering and application engineering, the variability model, and variation points/variants as first-class artefacts.
- ✅ **[1994]** [Case-Based Reasoning: Foundational Issues, Methodological Variations, and System Approaches](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.5555/196108.196115) — Agnar Aamodt et al.. The canonical CBR framework, including the R4 cycle - Retrieve, Reuse, Revise, Retain - and the treatment of a case base as an evolving, sustained-learning memory of concrete prior episodes with their solutions and outcomes.
- ✅ **[1988]** [gIBIS: A Hypertext Tool for Exploratory Policy Discussion](https://doi.org/10.1145/58566.59297) — Jeff Conklin et al.. A hypertext system implementing IBIS (Issue-Based Information Systems) to capture design deliberation as an explicit typed network of Issues, Positions and Arguments, aimed at preserving early design rationale on large complex problems.

## SQ1 · Selective Intervention

*When and how an agentic assessment system should request human judgment.*

- ✅ **[2026]** [Iterative vs. Plan-Driven Approaches in Human-LLM Collaborative Modeling: An Empirical Study](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-28117-3_1) — Iris Reinhartz-Berger et al.. An empirical comparison of two interaction regimes for human-LLM collaborative modeling: iterative (interleaved, incremental human-LLM turns) versus plan-driven (up-front specification then execution).
- ✅ **[2026]** The impact of LLM-generated models on novice domain modelers: a comparative experiment — Maxim Bragilovski et al.. A controlled experiment on how exposure to LLM-generated models affects novice domain modelers' own modeling outcomes.
- ✅ **[2025]** [Budgeted Multiple-Expert Deferral](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26706) — Giulia DeSalvo et al.. Attacks the TRAINING-TIME cost of deferral: standard deferral training queries every expert on every training instance.
- ✅ **[2025]** [Can Multimodal Large Language Models Grade Like an Expert? A Study on UML Class Diagram Assessment Accuracy](https://doi.org/10.1002/cae.70080) — María Blanca Ibáñez et al.. Empirical head-to-head: 34 engineering students' UML class diagrams (a SOLID-principles design task) graded independently by three expert instructors and four multimodal LLMs (ChatGPT-4, Gemini, Amazon AI, Claude 3.5 Sonnet).
- ✅ **[2025]** [Is Uncertainty Quantification a Viable Alternative to Learned Deferral?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02319) — Anna M. Wundram et al.. Head-to-head comparison of the two escalation paradigms - learned deferral (supervised, trained on the deployment distribution) versus threshold-on-uncertainty-quantification - finding UQ methods more robust under distribution shift and out-of-distribution input.
- ✅ **[2025]** [Know Your Limits: A Survey of Abstention in Large Language Models](https://doi.org/10.1162/tacl_a_00754) — Bingbing Wen et al.. A three-perspective framework for LLM abstention - the QUERY, the MODEL, and HUMAN VALUES - plus an organised inventory of abstention methods, benchmarks and evaluation metrics, with stated limitations of prior work.
- ⚠️ **[2025]** [Learning Paradigms for Hybrid Decision-Making Systems (arXiv title: AI, Meet Human: Learning Paradigms for Hybrid Decision Making Systems)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3802522) — Clara Punzi et al.. A recent taxonomy of hybrid decision-making systems with explicit attention to uncertainty-driven abstention mechanisms that determine when an AI acts autonomously versus when it calls for human intervention.
- ✅ **[2025]** [Learning to Ask: When LLM Agents Meet Unclear Instruction](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1104/) — Wenxuan Wang et al.. Documents that next-token-prediction training pushes LLM agents to FABRICATE missing arguments rather than ask, and proposes Ask-when-Needed plus the ToolEvaluator harness and Noisy ToolBench, evaluating both accuracy and interaction efficiency.
- ✅ **[2025]** [MCeT: Behavioral Model Correctness Evaluation using Large Language Models](https://doi.org/10.1109/MODELS67397.2025.00014) — Khaled E. Ahmed et al.. First fully automated LLM-based evaluation of a behavioural model (UML sequence diagrams) against free-text requirements, producing natural-language issue lists.
- ✅ **[2025]** [Toward Automated UML Diagram Assessment: Comparing LLM-Generated Scores with Teaching Assistants](https://doi.org/10.5220/0013481900003932) — Nacir Bouali et al.. Compares grades from three teaching assistants against three LLMs (Llama, GPT o1-mini, Claude) on 92 real student UML class diagram submissions, after converting diagrams to textual descriptions.
- ✅ **[2025]** [Towards Human-in-the-Loop LLM-Enabled Domain Modeling](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-08623-5_7) — Jonathan Silva Mercado et al.. Proposes an LLM-based modeling method with a human in the loop, combining automatic model creation with human supervision and interaction to refine and validate the model.
- ⚠️ **[2024]** [A User Study on Explainable Online Reinforcement Learning for Adaptive Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.04098 (preprint)) — Andreas Metzger et al.. A user study of whether explanations of online reinforcement learning decisions actually help humans supervise a self-adaptive system.
- ✅ **[2024]** [Conformal Alignment: Knowing When to Trust Foundation Models with Guarantees](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10301) — Yu Gui et al.. A framework that selects the subset of model outputs meeting a user-specified alignment criterion, guaranteeing that a prescribed fraction of SELECTED units genuinely meet it - model-agnostic and distribution-free - by training a lightweight alignment predictor on modest reference data.
- ✅ **[2024]** [Conformal Language Modeling](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/31421b112e5f7faf4fc577b74e45dab2-Abstract-Conference.html) — Victor Quach et al.. Extends conformal prediction to open-ended generation: calibrates a stopping rule for sampling LM outputs, growing a candidate set until the model is confident the set contains at least one acceptable response, with distribution-free statistical guarantees.
- ⚠️ **[2024]** [Cost-Sensitive Learning to Defer to Multiple Experts with Workload Constraints](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06906) — Jean V. Alves et al.. The DeCCaF method: deferral under (a) asymmetric error costs, (b) only one human prediction available per training instance rather than all experts labelling everything, and (c) hard per-expert work-capacity limits, solved with constraint programming.
- ✅ **[2024]** [How are LLMs Used for Conceptual Modeling? An Exploratory Study on Interaction Behavior and User Perception](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-75872-0_14) — Syed Juned Ali et al.. Empirical study of 76 undergraduate IS students in 39 groups doing UML use case and class (domain) modelling with LLM assistance.
- ✅ **[2024]** [Machine learning with a reject option: a survey](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10994-024-06534-x) — Kilian Hendrickx et al.. Consolidated taxonomy of rejection: AMBIGUITY rejection (the item sits near a decision boundary) vs NOVELTY rejection (the item is unlike anything seen), plus architectures (separated, dependent, integrated) and training strategies for each.
- ✅ **[2024]** [On the Utility of Domain Modeling Assistance with Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12577) — Meriem Ben Chaaben et al.. A study of the actual usefulness of LLM-plus-few-shot-prompting assistance for domain modelling, deliberately avoiding the need for large domain-specific training corpora, and evaluating whether modellers benefit from the generated suggestions across several modelling activities.
- ✅ **[2024]** [Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 laying down harmonised rules on artificial intelligence (Artificial Intelligence Act), Article 14 - Human oversight](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=OJ:L_202401689) — European Parliament and Council of the European Union. Legally requires high-risk AI systems to be designed so natural persons can EFFECTIVELY oversee them, with oversight measures COMMENSURATE with the risks, level of autonomy and context of use, and with overseers enabled to understand the system's capacities and limitations and to detect anomalies.
- ✅ **[2023]** [Conversational Process Modelling: State of the Art, Applications, and Implications in Practice](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-41623-1_19) — Nataliia Klievtsova et al.. Systematic analysis of existing chatbots for conversational process modelling, evaluated on real higher-education process datasets, with application scenarios positioned along the process life cycle plus practical guidance and open research needs.
- ✅ **[2023]** [Human-in-the-loop machine learning: a state of the art](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10462-022-10246-w) — Eduardo Mosqueira-Rey et al.. Organises HITL-ML by WHO HOLDS CONTROL of the learning loop: active learning (system initiates), interactive machine learning (tight bidirectional loop), machine teaching (human domain expert initiates and controls).
- ✅ **[2023]** [Robots That Ask For Help: Uncertainty Alignment for Large Language Model Planners](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01928) — Allen Z. Ren et al.. KnowNo: uses conformal prediction to calibrate an LLM planner's uncertainty so it asks a human for help exactly when needed, with a statistical guarantee on task success while MINIMISING the amount of human help requested.
- ✅ **[2023]** [Semantic Uncertainty: Linguistic Invariances for Uncertainty Estimation in Natural Language Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09664) — Lorenz Kuhn et al.. Token-level entropy misestimates uncertainty because semantically equivalent sentences are counted as distinct outcomes.
- ✅ **[2023]** [Who Should Predict? Exact Algorithms For Learning to Defer to Humans](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v206/mozannar23a/mozannar23a.pdf) — Hussein Mozannar et al.. Shows prior surrogate losses can fail to find the system-optimal deferral rule, gives exact/consistent algorithms for jointly learning classifier and rejector so the AI COMPLEMENTS the human, and contributes a human-AI deferral evaluation benchmark.
- ✅ **[2022]** [A survey of human-in-the-loop for machine learning](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.future.2022.05.014) — Xingjiao Wu et al.. Classifies HITL work by intervention point - data processing, interventional model training, and system-independent HITL design - and frames the field's objective explicitly as reaching target accuracy at MINIMUM HUMAN COST.
- ✅ **[2022]** [Detecting mistakes in a domain model](https://doi.org/10.1145/3550356.3561583) — Prabhsimran Singh et al.. Automated detection of mistakes in student-produced domain models, with the extended version comparing three detection approaches head to head.
- ✅ **[2022]** [Language Models (Mostly) Know What They Know](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05221) — Saurav Kadavath et al.. Large models are reasonably well-calibrated on multiple-choice and true/false questions when the format is right, and can be prompted to self-evaluate (P(True)) and to predict whether they know the answer (P(IK)).
- ✅ **[2022]** [The flaws of policies requiring human oversight of government algorithms](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3921216) — Ben Green. Surveys 41 policies mandating human oversight of government algorithms and identifies two structural flaws: people cannot in practice perform the oversight functions the policies assume, and the oversight requirement legitimises deployment of faulty systems without fixing them.
- ✅ **[2021]** [Does the Whole Exceed its Parts? The Effect of AI Explanations on Complementary Team Performance](https://doi.org/10.1145/3411764.3445717) — Gagan Bansal et al.. AI augmentation produced complementary team improvement, but explanations did NOT further increase it - explanations tended to increase acceptance of AI recommendations regardless of correctness rather than improve discrimination.
- ✅ **[2020]** [Consistent Estimators for Learning to Defer to an Expert](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/mozannar20b.html) — Hussein Mozannar et al.. A consistent surrogate loss for learning to defer, via reduction to cost-sensitive learning, that generalises cross-entropy - trained using only observed samples of the expert's decisions, without needing a model of the expert's internal process.
- ✅ **[2020]** [Is automated grading of models effective? Assessing automated grading of class diagrams](https://doi.org/10.1145/3365438.3410944) — Weiyi Bian et al.. Empirical evaluation of automated model grading in two real courses - a beginner class of 103 students producing an object-oriented design model and an advanced class of 89 students producing a domain model - assessing where automated grades agree with and diverge from human instructors.
- ✅ **[2019]** [Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction](https://doi.org/10.1145/3290605.3300233) — Saleema Amershi et al.. 18 design guidelines for human-AI interaction, synthesised from 150+ recommendations and validated through a modified-heuristic-evaluation user study with 49 design practitioners against 20 AI-infused products.
- ✅ **[2019]** [Proactive Process Adaptation Using Deep Learning Ensembles](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-21290-2_34) — Andreas Metzger et al.. An ensemble of deep learning models that emits a reliability estimate alongside each prediction, and uses that estimate to dynamically determine the earliest point at which a prediction is accurate enough to justify a proactive adaptation.
- ✅ **[2019]** [The Principles and Limits of Algorithm-in-the-Loop Decision Making](https://scholar.harvard.edu/bgreen/publications/principles-and-limits-algorithm-loop-decision-making) — Ben Green et al.. Empirically shows people do not collaborate with algorithms the way normative accounts assume - people cannot reliably evaluate algorithmic predictions, do not correctly calibrate their reliance, and society lacks clear normative principles for what good human-algorithm collaboration even means.
- ✅ **[2018]** [Predict Responsibly: Improving Fairness and Accuracy by Learning to Defer](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.06664) — David Madras et al.. Distinguishes REJECTION (abstain in isolation) from DEFERRAL (abstain knowing a specific downstream decision-maker will act), and shows the optimal abstention policy changes once you model the human who receives the deferred case - including when that human is inconsistent or biased.
- ✅ **[2017]** [Effects of workload, work complexity, and repeated alerts on alert fatigue in a clinical decision support system](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12911-017-0430-8) — Jessica S. Ancker et al.. Empirically separates two mechanisms of alert fatigue - cognitive overload (from work volume, complexity, and difficulty telling informative from uninformative alerts) and desensitisation (from repeated exposure to the same alert over time) - using longitudinal clinician-level data.
- ✅ **[2017]** [Selective Classification for Deep Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08500) — Yonatan Geifman et al.. A practical procedure (SGR) for wrapping an already-trained deep network with a selective classifier that guarantees a user-specified risk level with high probability at maximal coverage, using a held-out calibration set.
- ✅ **[2016]** [Learning with Rejection](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-46379-7_5) — Corinna Cortes et al.. Shows that the rejector should be LEARNED JOINTLY with the predictor rather than derived post-hoc from confidence, with Rademacher-complexity generalisation bounds, consistency and calibration results for the resulting surrogate losses.
- ✅ **[2010]** [On the Foundations of Noise-free Selective Classification](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume11/el-yaniv10a/el-yaniv10a.pdf) — Ran El-Yaniv et al.. Formalises selective classification as an explicit coverage/risk tradeoff, and introduces the risk-coverage curve as the evaluation object.
- ✅ **[2009]** [Active Learning Literature Survey](https://minds.wisconsin.edu/handle/1793/60660) — Burr Settles. The canonical catalogue of query strategies - uncertainty sampling, query-by-committee, expected model change, expected error reduction, variance reduction, density-weighting - and the three query scenarios (pool-based, stream-based, membership synthesis).
- ✅ **[2006]** [Overriding of Drug Safety Alerts in Computerized Physician Order Entry](https://doi.org/10.1197/jamia.M1809) — Heleen van der Sijs et al.. Systematic review finding clinicians override drug safety alerts in 49% to 96% of cases, and attributing this to error-producing conditions in the alerting system itself: low specificity, low sensitivity, unclear information content, and unnecessary workflow disruption.
- ⚠️ **[2005]** [Diagram Matching for Human-Computer Collaborative Assessment](https://caaconference.lboro.ac.uk/pastConferences/2005/proceedings/TselonisC_SargeantJ_McGee_WoodM.pdf (repository record: https://repository.lboro.ac.uk/articles/conference_contribution/Diagram_matching_for_human-computer_collaborative_assessment/9488852)) — Christos Tselonis et al.. Methods for matching student-drawn diagrams against reference diagrams, applied explicitly within a HUMAN-COMPUTER COLLABORATIVE ASSESSMENT paradigm: the machine finds similarities using a simple heuristic process (reported effective) and the human marker adjudicates the rest.
- ✅ **[1999]** [Principles of Mixed-Initiative User Interfaces](https://doi.org/10.1145/302979.303030) — Eric Horvitz. Twelve principles for coupling direct manipulation with interface agents, centred on considering the uncertainty of the user's goals and the user's attention, and introducing automation only where it has genuine expected value over direct manipulation.
- ✅ **[1970]** [On optimum recognition error and reject tradeoff](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1970.1054406) — C. K. Chow. The original Bayes-optimal reject rule: abstain when the maximum posterior falls below a threshold, and the error-reject tradeoff curve that this induces.

## SQ2 · Governed Knowledge Reuse

*Representing, validating, reconciling and storing expert judgment.*

- ✅ **[2025]** [Leveraging LLMs for Domain Modeling: The Impact of Granularity and Strategy on Quality](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-94569-4_1) — Iris Reinhartz-Berger et al.. That the granularity at which a modeling task is decomposed, and the prompting strategy used, materially affect the quality of LLM-produced domain models.
- ✅ **[2025]** [Rethinking machine unlearning for large language models](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-025-00985-0) — Sijia Liu et al.. Reframes unlearning specifically for LLMs - unlearning scope, data-model interaction, and multifaceted efficacy assessment - and highlights that the boundaries of what has actually been removed are poorly defined and hard to verify in large models.
- ✅ **[2025]** [Towards Multi-Agentic AI for automated software design and modelling: challenges and opportunities](https://doi.org/10.1109/ASEW67777.2025.00063) — Hoa Khanh Dam. A vision/position paper proposing a conceptual multi-agentic AI framework of autonomous goal-driven agents for software design, and naming the open challenges explicitly: translating natural-language requirements into design models, resolving ambiguities, maintaining consistency within and between design models, and managing conflicts and inconsistencies when merging different versions of a design model.
- ✅ **[2024]** [A Survey on the Memory Mechanism of Large Language Model based Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13501) — Zeyu Zhang et al.. A systematic taxonomy of LLM-agent memory covering sources, forms, and the memory operations of writing, management and reading, together with evaluation approaches and open problems.
- ✅ **[2024]** [Embedding-based Automated Assessment of Domain Models](https://doi.org/10.1145/3652620.3687774) — Kua Chen et al.. Uses embedding-based semantic similarity to compare student-generated domain models against a reference solution for automated assessment at scale, addressing the manual-grading bottleneck as software engineering enrolments grow.
- ✅ **[2024]** [Generative AI And Software Variability - A Research Vision](https://doi.org/10.1145/3634713.3634722) — Sandra Greiner et al.. A community research agenda for the intersection of generative AI and software variability, written by several of the field's leading variability researchers.
- ✅ **[2024]** [Multi-step Iterative Automated Domain Modeling with Large Language Models](https://doi.org/10.1145/3652620.3687807) — Yujing Yang et al.. Criticises single-shot prompting (feeding all information to the LLM in one step) and proposes a multi-step framework that extracts model elements - classes, attributes, relationships - stepwise, INJECTING INSTRUCTIONS AND HUMAN KNOWLEDGE AT EACH STEP, with an iterative pattern-identification loop and a SELF-REFLECTION mechanism that generates internal feedback and folds it back into the domain model.
- ✅ **[2024]** [Towards an Interoperable Model-driven Automated Assessment System for Computer Science Education](https://doi.org/10.1145/3652620.3687775) — Markus Hamann et al.. Argues that the automated-assessment landscape in CS education is fragmented into siloed, non-interoperable systems, and proposes a model-driven architecture with common exchange representations so assessment logic and artefacts can move between systems.
- ⚠️ **[2023]** [Contestable AI by Design: Towards a Framework](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11023-022-09611-z) — Kars Alfrink et al.. Synthesises from the literature a design framework for contestable AI - systems built to remain responsive to human intervention throughout their lifecycle, with mechanisms for challenging and revising automated decisions rather than only explaining them.
- ✅ **[2023]** [Generative Agents: Interactive Simulacra of Human Behavior](https://doi.org/10.1145/3586183.3606763) — Joon Sung Park et al.. An agent memory architecture with three components - a time-stamped memory stream of natural-language observations, a retrieval function scoring recency/importance/relevance, and reflection that synthesises higher-level abstractions from stored observations - validated by ablation showing each component is necessary.
- ✅ **[2023]** [Measuring Attribution in Natural Language Generation Models](https://doi.org/10.1162/coli_a_00486) — Hannah Rashkin et al.. The AIS (Attributable to Identified Sources) evaluation framework, which defines when generated text is genuinely supported by a provided source, plus a two-stage human annotation pipeline for applying that judgment reliably.
- ✅ **[2023]** [MemGPT: Towards LLMs as Operating Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08560) — Charles Packer et al.. Virtual context management by analogy to OS hierarchical memory: an explicit tiered store (in-context 'main' memory vs out-of-context 'external' memory) with the LLM issuing its own function calls to page information in and out and to write persistent records.
- ✅ **[2023]** [On the assessment of generative AI in modeling tasks: an experience report with ChatGPT and UML](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10270-023-01105-5) — Javier Cámara et al.. Widely-cited experience report showing that, unlike code generation, ChatGPT's performance on software modelling tasks is limited: syntactic and semantic deficiencies, lack of consistency across responses to the same prompt, and scalability problems.
- ✅ **[2023]** [Open Problems and Fundamental Limitations of Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15217) — Stephen Casper et al.. A systematic taxonomy of RLHF's failure modes, separating tractable problems from fundamental limitations, and explicitly calls for auditing, transparency and documentation standards around human feedback.
- ✅ **[2023]** [Toward a Perspectivist Turn in Ground Truthing for Predictive Computing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.04270) — Federico Cabitza et al.. Argues for 'data perspectivism' as a paradigm: ground truth datasets should integrate and retain the multiple perspectives of human annotators rather than collapsing them into a single gold standard via majority voting.
- ✅ **[2022]** [A conceptual model for unifying variability in space and time: Rationale, validation, and illustrative applications](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10664-021-10097-z) — Sofia Ananieva et al.. ## Taxonomy

Two taxonomies are in play, and this project uses both deliberately rather than treating one as a replacement for the other.

**The Judgment Lifecycle Grid** (this project, see the course-presentation classification framework) follows *one expert ruling* through five checkpoints: `TRIGGER → ASK → RECORD → REUSE → PROVE`. Each reviewed stream is coded F (sustained), P (partial) or A (not addressed) per checkpoint. The two weakest handoffs in the current corpus are `ASK → RECORD` and `RECORD → REUSE`.

**Zou et al. 2026's taxonomy** (the published, peer-reviewed reference point) classifies LLM-based human-agent systems along environment profiling, human-feedback mechanisms (type, granularity, phase), **interaction mode** (supervision, cooperation, coordination, delegation), orchestration strategy, and communication.

**Open reconciliation task:** the Judgment Lifecycle Grid's checkpoints and Zou et al.'s interaction modes describe overlapping ground from different angles and have not yet been cross-mapped. Doing so before the grid is presented as a novel contribution is one of the concrete next steps this index surfaces — not yet done, so no claim of novelty relative to the published taxonomy is made here.

## Contributing an entry

1. Verify the source independently — publisher page, DOI, dblp or arXiv record. A generated citation is not a verified one.
2. Add a record to `verified-research-corpus-2026-08-12.json` with `verification` set honestly (`VERIFIED_ONLINE` only if you actually checked); tag it to the RQ it serves.
3. Rerun `python scripts/build_awesome_literature_index.py` to regenerate this file.
4. If a source cannot be verified, it goes in the Needs Checking section and is never cited elsewhere — do not quietly drop the verification step under time pressure.
